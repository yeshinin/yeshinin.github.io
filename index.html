Agentic AI for Health

OTCSafe: AI for Self-Medication Risk Detection

by Yeshi Dolma

A conversational AI agent that detects harmful over-the-counter self-medication behaviors and explains associated risks in plain language.

Read more

Type anything — “I take NyQuil and Tylenol every night with wine” — and the agent identifies dangerous drug combinations, duplicate ingredient exposure, dose limit exceedances, and drug-supplement interactions. Every finding is cited to its source.

  • Resolves brand names to active ingredients (NyQuil → acetaminophen + dextromethorphan + ...)
  • Detects pairwise interactions via OpenFDA label search
  • Reasons pharmacologically using Claude to catch interactions databases miss — serotonin syndrome, CYP450 enzyme interactions, stimulant stacking, CNS depression combinations, duplicate therapeutic classes
  • Accumulates ingredient doses across multiple products and flags exceedances of safe daily limits
  • Writes plain-language risk reports with severity ratings and actionable advice
  • Cites every finding to a named source with a URL

Not a substitute for professional medical advice.

Computational Epidemiology

COVID-19 Misinformation & Behavioral Signals

by Yeshi Dolma

Mapping Misinformation Search Trends Against COVID-19 Case Dynamics (King County & Washington State)

Read more

This project investigates how misinformation evolves during a public health crisis—and more importantly, what actually drives its spread. Rather than treating misinformation as a static content problem, I approached it as a dynamic behavioral signal shaped by policy, communication, and institutional trust.

  • Causal Analysis Pipeline: Modeled Google Trends as a proxy for misinformation engagement (e.g., vaccine denial, institutional skepticism) and correlated composite indices with COVID-19 trajectories in Washington.
  • Signal Processing & Contextualization: Built workflows for smoothing, normalization, and policy-event annotation to isolate drivers of misinformation surges.
  • Mechanism Identification: Revealed that misinformation engagement was primarily driven by policy and communication vacuums (e.g., CDC mask guidance shifts, vaccine rollout gaps) and guidance reversals, while remaining largely decoupled from disease severity; institutional skepticism persisted due to cumulative credibility erosion.
  • Policy-Relevant Insights: Research indicated that interventions require targeting institutional communication and behavioral strategies over traditional models focused solely on addressing disease-based information gaps.
  • Ongoing Work: Extending with NLP (topic modeling, sentiment analysis) to track narrative evolution and cross-state variation.

About

healthcare is a human right.

I am driven by the belief that without health security, human potential is unfairly constrained; equitable health must come first so anyone can dream.

As an Applied ML Scientist with over nine years of experience building large-scale AI systems, my work sits at the intersection of machine learning, AI and improving public health outcomes. Currently focused on advanced predictive modeling, generative AI, and causal inference to identify mechanisms driving public health patterns and generate equity-centered, policy-relevant evidence and decision support in resource-constrained settings.

Interests: Predictive and counterfactual modeling; generative AI; resource optimization; spatiotemporal systems; behavioral health analytics; global health; women’s health; cancer care continuum; bias-aware causal inference; and scalable AI for low-resource public health systems.
